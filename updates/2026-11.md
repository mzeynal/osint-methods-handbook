## AI provenance checks (S)

- **SynthID** is an invisible mark that Google DeepMind embeds in images, video, audio and text produced by its models. On 7 October 2026 Google opened SynthID Detector to all users in the English version; before that it was a waiting-list check for journalists and researchers (S).
- **Others joining.** In May 2026 OpenAI (expanding its use), Kakao and ElevenLabs joined SynthID; OpenAI introduced a preview public tool for checking images made in ChatGPT and through its API (S).
- **Google I/O (19–20 May 2026).** Adding SynthID verification and C2PA detection to Chrome and Search was announced; mark checking on images works in a number of Google features, and for video and audio it is available through the Gemini app (S).
- **TikTok**, per the C2PA coalition, joined the steering committee on 28 July 2026 and reports more than three billion items labelled "made with AI" (S).

## Satellite imagery access (S)

- After the war with Iran began (28 February), Planet Labs introduced a four-day delay in publishing imagery of the Middle East, raised it to two weeks on 10 March, and on 4 April announced a "managed access model": imagery of the region is withheld indefinitely (from 9 March) and released on individual request for urgent tasks or in the public interest. The company said the US government had asked all providers to introduce an indefinite hold voluntarily.
- Vantor (formerly Maxar) said the government had not contacted it, but that it has long reserved the right to introduce "enhanced access controls" during conflicts and applies them in part of the region.
- Commentators note the unprecedented scale and duration of the restrictions: since 1992 US operators have been licensed by NOAA, but 2020 amendments narrowed the levers of control.
- Despite the restrictions, some imagery reached circulation through the media and analysts: Airbus (Pleiades Neo), SkyFi and Satellogic; the presence of the Chinese companies Jilin-1 and Siwei is growing.

## Beneficial ownership, registers and sanctions (S)

- **US.** On 11 August 2026 FinCEN adopted a final rule: domestic US companies and US citizens are exempt from beneficial ownership reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act (in force from 14 August); reporting remains for foreign companies registered in the US, for their non-US beneficial owners. There is no federal register of owners of US companies.
- **United Kingdom.** From 18 November 2025 Companies House requires identity verification of directors and persons with significant control; the transition period for existing individuals ends on 17–18 November 2026 (sources give both dates).
- **EU.** The beneficial ownership provisions of Chapter IV of the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (AMLR) apply from 10 July 2027.
- **Sanctions.** From 28 January 2026 the single UK Sanctions List replaced the separate OFSI list; the EU adopted its 20th (23 April), 21st (23 July) and 22nd (12 October) packages against Russia, the 22nd with 1,569 listings (743 individuals and 826 entities); FATF added Bosnia and Herzegovina and Iraq to the grey list in June 2026 and removed Algeria and Namibia, and at its plenary of 26–30 October removed DR Congo, Monaco, Bulgaria and Côte d'Ivoire with no additions (the grey list now has 18 jurisdictions; the black list is unchanged).
- **Crypto.** Wallet addresses have been on sanctions lists since 2018; in 2025 sanctions were imposed on Grinex (successor of the Garantex exchange) and the related rouble stablecoin A7A5.

## Regional platform example: Xiaohongshu (S)

Published guides to the platform are a useful starting point, but the figures below were taken from secondary summaries.

Bellingcat's guide, published on 20 April 2026, describes Xiaohongshu as a rare window into Chinese society for researchers outside the country. The main findings:

- **Scale.** The platform was founded in 2013 as a Hong Kong shopping guide and reportedly has about 300 million monthly active users; by the end of 2024 it accounted for around 600 million search queries a day, roughly half of Baidu's. For many users it is a search engine.
- **Audience and topics.** Mainly young urban women (in 2024 about 70% of users were women and about half were Generation Z). It suits investigations into consumer fraud, diaspora life, women's issues, fake reviews and scams: for example newsrooms have used posts to expose a grey market in paid reviews; in one case a serial offender in the UK was convicted after a victim posted a warning on the platform.
- **Political censorship.** There is little political and current news: the platform answers to the Cyberspace Administration; in 2022 a leaked internal document showed how moderators block sensitive topics. So findings reflect only what has "survived" censorship.
- **Access.** In international app stores the app is called RedNote; accounts and content are shared. Users registered with a foreign number are marked as international, and the algorithm shows them a different, "curated" slice: foreign bloggers, comparisons of China with the West.
- **Language.** Automatic translation does not translate search queries; a search in English gives one slice, in simplified Chinese another. For the real picture use queries in Chinese (translate the query if needed) and, where possible, work with a native speaker.
- **Slang.** Users replace words with emoji and codes to get around filters (for example "vx" for WeChat); without a native speaker the meaning can be missed.
- **Search.** Basic filters by time and location; for advanced analysis, paid marketing-analytics services show trends and key authors; interest group chats are also useful.
- **Preservation.** Take a full-screen capture of the post with the address, time, name, counters and labels; archive the page; **save the unique profile identifier from the address**: the name can change, the identifier stays.
- **Safety.** The platform must comply with authorities' data requests; posts on sensitive topics may harm users.
